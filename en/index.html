Real Go, with a little magic

Mặc Linh plays by standard rules: area scoring and komi 7.5, on 9×9, 13×13 or 19×19 boards. The spirits are only a layer of painting. They never add or remove a single point; they just make sure you notice the shapes that decide games.

  • Dragon — a long connected group grows into a dragon. Big, strong, and one shared set of liberties.
  • Triple Ko — three kos in a cycle; retaking would repeat a past position, so it's forbidden.
  • Twins — seki: both groups are locked, and whoever moves first dies.
  • Tiger — a tiger's mouth: three stones guarding one point. Play inside and you get eaten.
  • Bamboo — a bamboo joint that nobody can cut.
  • Tortoise — a shell that closes around two eyes.
  • Plum Blossom — a ponnuki: four stones bloom after a capture.
  • Divine Eye — a group with two real eyes lives forever.
  • Two-Headed Snake — the eyes look false, yet the group lives because its stones form a ring.
  • Tesuji replays — ladders, snapbacks and false eyes are replayed move by move with a short explanation.

The Tale of Go

New to Go? Start with The Tale of Go, a story mode told by a teahouse storyteller. Legend says Emperor Yao invented Go to teach his restless son Danzhu, who would not sit still for any lesson. You learn alongside the boy, from your very first stone.

The first three chapters cover the four basic rules, eyes and life, and the classic shapes. Each chapter ends with a challenge game against Danzhu: a capture race, a two-stone handicap game, then an even game.

How to play Go in one minute

  1. Black and White take turns placing one stone on an empty intersection. Stones never move.
  2. The empty points next to a group are its liberties, its breath. A group with no liberties left is captured.
  3. You may not play a stone that leaves your own group without liberties (unless it captures).
  4. Ko: you may not immediately recapture a single stone if that would repeat the previous position.
  5. At the end, your score is your stones plus the empty points you surround. White gets 7.5 points of komi for moving second.

The Tale of Go teaches each rule with a small puzzle, so you don't need to memorise this list.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There are no ads and nothing to buy. On itch.io you can leave a donation if you like.

Do I need an account?

No. Open the page and play. Your progress is saved in your browser.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes, the browser version works on phones and tablets. For the strongest computer opponent, use the Windows build.

Which rules does it use?

Standard rules with area scoring and komi 7.5. The spirits are decoration only and never change the score.

Windows says the file is unrecognised.

The game is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may warn you. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".

Was AI used to make it?

Some of the art and text were made with the help of generative AI tools. This is disclosed on the itch.io page.
